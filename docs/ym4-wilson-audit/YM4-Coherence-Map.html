YM4 Coherence Map

This map compares a conditional weak-coupling construction of four-dimensional Yang–Mills theory with a proposed strong-coupling expansion. The first aims to remove the lattice cutoff; the second aims to control long-distance decay. Joining them requires control of the full effective action through the intermediate regime, plus compatible observables and transfer spaces.

These findings do not refute unseen later fragment proofs, but they prevent promoting the coefficient result on the evidence available here.

A six-face cube is an exact counterexample

Lemma 2.3 of the recovered FALLBACK_LOWER_ORDER_ACTIVITY_1_proof_v1.md requires an even count of active edges at every surviving surface vertex. The boundary of an elementary cube has six faces, twelve edges and eight vertices. Every edge belongs to two faces, while each vertex has three active edges. Nevertheless it survives Haar integration.

∫ U_ij conjugate(U_kl) dU = δ_ik δ_jl / 2 cube integral of six ordinary fundamental characters = 2^8 / 2^12 = 1/16

The exact checker constructs the oriented faces, pairs the twelve link matrices and counts eight remaining index loops. The valid elementary parity condition is on representation factors at an integrated link. It does not imply even vertex degree.

Character factors and Haar normalization must be fixed together

For ordinary SU(2) characters, the vacuum-normalized Wilson coefficient is (a+1)Ia+1(x)/I₁(x). The early note instead uses Ia+1/I₁ as its face coefficient. For a fundamental cube the correct activity is (2t₁)⁶/16 = 4t₁⁶, whereas its ordinary-character formula gives t₁⁶/16. If an unstated dimension-weighted character convention is intended, the coefficient can be repaired, but the cube Haar integral becomes 4 and violates the note’s separate |IHaar| ≤ 1 bound.

A local projector having operator norm one is not enough to bound a closed tensor contraction by one: closing indices can introduce its trace and representation dimensions. The recovered v1.15a audit already flags the missing identification with the physical Wilson transfer operator. A finite rank scan in v1.15b does not by itself establish the complete global domination theorem.

An independent conservative Wilson theorem

Use the actual measure exp[xΣp tr Up] times product Haar measure, with normalized trace tr = Tr/2. A link in four dimensions belongs to six plaquettes, each containing three other links. The checker reconstructs these eighteen influence incidences.

Changing one neighboring link changes the single-link log density by a function whose oscillation is at most 4x per shared plaquette. A probability tilt by exp(h) changes total variation by at most tanh(osc(h)/4). Thus a shared plaquette contributes at most tanh x to the conditional influence, giving row sum α ≤ 18 tanh x.

α = 18 tanh(x) < 1 0 < x < atanh(1/18) = (1/2)log(19/17) 0.0556128 < atanh(1/18) < 0.0556129 |Cov(F,G)| ≤ ||δF||₁ ||δG||₁ α^r / [4(1−α)]

Here δeF is the maximum change in a bounded real observable F when only link e changes, and r is the distance between the supports in the graph joining links that share a plaquette. The standard Dobrushin comparison theorem gives uniqueness, boundary-independent thermodynamic convergence and the covariance bound. The proof note derives the covariance estimate by a local source tilt and bounds paths using (I−C)⁻¹. At x = 0.05, α lies between 0.89925074924183949 and 0.89925074924183951.

This is a conservative baseline for the physical measure, with no new optimality claim. Its range is smaller than the Shen–Zhu–Zhu window cited in the original map. The graph-distance rate has not been promoted here to a physical Hamiltonian gap.

1. Fix the weights and the recoupling convention

r_a(x) = (a+1) I_(a+1)(x)/I_1(x); ξ_a = r_a² ξ_a(τ) = (a+1)² exp[−τ a(a+2)/4] Q₂ = Σ ξ_a² Q₃ = Σ_(ordered admissible triples) ξ_a ξ_b ξ_c Q₄ = Σ_(ordered quadruples with a common channel) ξ_a ξ_b ξ_c ξ_d ρ_act = e [39 Q₂ + 95(Q₃ + Q₄)]

All labels in these sums are positive doubled spins. A quadruple is counted once when a channel exists. For example, (1,1,1,1) has common channels 0 and 2, but is counted once. This convention reproduces the uploaded numbers; checking that multiplicities are handled correctly in the actual expansion is a separate proof obligation. The heat-kernel convention above corresponds to coefficients (a+1) exp[−τ a(a+2)/8] before squaring.

2. Replace a truncation check with an infinite-tail bound

The positive Bessel series proves In+1/In ≤ x/[2(n+1)], hence ξa+1/ξa ≤ x²/[4(a+1)²]. After cutoff M, the first omitted weight and that ratio give a geometric bound T on Σa>M ξa. If S bounds the full weight sum, the discarded contributions to Q₂, Q₃ and Q₄ are at most T², 3TS² and 4TS³. Dropping admissibility restrictions only enlarges these positive bounds.

The certificate uses 100-digit integer grids with outward rounding. Bessel and exponential remainders are bounded by positive series, and logarithms by the convergent atanh series. It requires only Python’s standard library. This is a runnable arithmetic certificate, not a formal verification of the entire physical argument.

3. State the density and spectral steps separately

Density: on a finite graph, Peter–Weyl approximation followed by gauge averaging gives dense spin-network combinations in gauge-invariant L² for any gauge-invariant probability measure. Increasing finite-edge conditional expectations extends this to local time-zero observables on an infinite spatial lattice. Orthonormality is a Haar-measure statement; it is not required for density.

Spectral step: let T be a positive self-adjoint contraction, TΩ = Ω, with Ω normalized. If a dense family has centered moments ⟨Qψ,TⁿQψ⟩ ≤ Cψe−mn, with one common m > 0 and constants independent of n, the spectral theorem excludes all spectrum above e−m on Ω⊥. Any positive spectral mass above that number would eventually exceed the assumed moment bound. Thus Ω is the unique invariant vector. For injective T, H = −log T has a gap at least m.

Applying this lemma requires identifying the time-zero observables with a dense family in the actual physical transfer space and checking the time step in the correlation estimate. A spatial activity rate does not itself establish that identification. The same issue remains for continuum observables and sectors not generated by them.

4. Strengthen the statement of the middle

For each fixed allowed reference coupling, seek a finite blocking count k* and a strict budget ρ* < 1 that are independent of the ultraviolet cutoff and the relevant volumes. The estimate must cover the full induced interactions and large-field histories. Compactness of a scalar coupling interval does not provide compactness or control of that family of effective actions.

Even a volume-uniform zero-free region would address analyticity rather than prove a mass gap or membership in this particular polymer basin. Small-box zero-free checks are exploratory unless uniform extension is proved. Likewise, a gap for bounded Wilson-loop observables does not by itself bound renormalized stress-tensor insertions for the rotation argument.

Next useful milestone: inspect the original strong-coupling expansion and verify that these coefficient bounds satisfy every step of its convergence proof. That would justify a Wilson clustering theorem. Estimating the full intermediate flow remains a later, much harder task.

The shared axis

Wilson conventions share an axis; effective actions need more coordinates

Both use the same coupling. Theorem 0 writes the Wilson weight as exp[−g−2 Σ(1 − Re tr U)] with tr 1 = 1, so its running inverse coupling x = g−2 is exactly the β in our Bessel coefficients ca = 2(a+1) Ia+1(β)/β. This identifies the Wilson normalization. Heat-kernel time τ is a different parameter: matching one character does not make the two actions identical. A blocked Wilson action also acquires additional interactions that this scalar axis does not display.

Positions are to scale except the left edge of the Theorem 0 band: the stopping coupling g is chosen small, below thresholds such as γ that are never given values. Shen–Zhu–Zhu write the action as Nβ Re Σ Tr Qp with the unnormalized trace, so for SU(2) their x is 4β and their bound β < 1/48 is x < 1/12. Our edge x* comes from the fallback grammar with Wilson weights (see the frontier section). One-loop step size c₀ = (11N²/12π²) log L is 0.41, 0.60 and 0.72 for L = 3, 5, 7 at N = 2. One loop says nothing reliable inside the middle, so it gives no honest count of the steps needed to cross it.

B2

The middle, stated as one obligation

open

Clause (0)(b) puts the final coupling in a compact window [g₋(g), g). So the middle can be posed as a statement about a compact family, uniform in the cutoff:

MIDDLE-1. Fix an allowed reference coupling g. There are k* < ∞ and ρ* < 1, independent of cutoff and required volumes, such that, for every effective density produced at the reference lattice with g_K ∈ [g₋(g), g), k* further blockings yield an effective action in the generalized fallback basin with ρ ≤ ρ* < 1.
  • This is the confinement crossover. Two problems sit inside it: the coupling has to go from weak to strong, and the action has to move from Bałaban's multi-scale localised terms into a species grammar our entropy bounds can count (our GENERALIZED_FALLBACK_BASIN_3).
  • Large-field histories make the effective density a sum of measures, not one Gibbs weight. Any certificate has to cover every history, or bound their total mass. Their own large-field rarity statements (risk register) are among the items still marked outstanding.
  • On the ladder the middle runs from x ≈ 0.63 up to g⁻². Its left end is now a computed number; its right end is not.

Use: keep this as the single named open link in both ledgers. Everything else on this page is support for it or a consequence of it.

B3

Their finite-size gap criterion turns the gap into one box

claimed in companion account

Their Outlook (§9.4) describes a criterion proved in the companion account. On a torus the partition function is the trace of a power of a positive transfer operator. A finite-size excess of the free energy then obeys a quadratic inequality under doubling of the box. If the excess sits below a universal threshold in one box, uniformly in the cutoff, it decays doubly exponentially along the doubled boxes, and every larger box has a unique ground state with a gap. On R⁴ this is a dichotomy: either the excess stays above threshold at every scale, or there is a mass gap.

They note the condition fails in every small box and would have to be checked at large scales their construction does not reach. Large scales are where the coupling is strong, which is where our machinery works.

GAP-CRITERION-CHECK-1 1. Evaluate the free-energy excess on one box for the τ = 5 fallback theory, using the polymer expansion to control log Z with certified error. 2. Confirm the criterion fires there (sanity check: a gap is already known in that regime). 3. Scan upward in x to find where a certifiable box stops existing. That edge is a measured frontier for the middle.

Use: the best concrete joint project. It needs their exact statement first: I searched the three TeX parts in the repo and did not find it, so it is probably in a part of the companion account they have not posted.

B4

A gap gives them O(4) and uniqueness

stated in their account
  • Their companion account lists sufficient conditions for the far-sphere flux hypothesis of clause (vi). Two of them are "a mass gap uniform in K" and "inf spec(H restricted to Ω⊥) > 0" together with ultraviolet boundedness of one stress-tensor insertion. They also state a proposition titled Scalar reflected decay from clustering or a mass gap.
  • The flux hypothesis needs stress-tensor correlations to decay faster than |x|−4; they note slower decay does not suffice, tree level gives |x|−8, and a gap gives exponential decay. A matching exponential estimate would supply the required long-distance decay only after the renormalized stress-tensor insertion and its ultraviolet bounds have been controlled.
  • Their §9.3 says uniqueness of the infinite-volume limit "needs infrared input, such as a last-scale cluster expansion in infinite volume or a gap uniform in the volume." Our BDE-CANCEL plus the boundary-defect Cauchy bound is that last-scale cluster expansion.
  • Their per-ball bounds in clause (iii)(b) hold with no decay. Composed with our expansion at the last scale they would pick up a factor ρdist.

Use: these are conditional payoffs. MIDDLE-1 must be supplemented by observable matching, physical time normalization, the relevant ultraviolet insertion bounds and the exact flux hypothesis. A gap for bounded lattice observables alone does not supply stress-tensor control.

B5

bookkeeping

Our gap is measured in blocks at the last scale. Their reference length is the physical unit, labelled by the renormalised coupling (clause v), and their one-loop law ties it to the cutoff through dimensional transmutation. So the field our schema calls mass_gap_conversion_scale is their ℓref:

Use: state every future gap number with its Lk*ℓref denominator so results from the two programs compare directly.

B6

Zero-free regions, uniform in the volume

their open questionour tool

Clause (v) labels the torus theories by a real-analytic parameter, but its constants degrade with the volume. They trace this to "the open question whether the partition functions are free of zeros near the real coupling axis uniformly in the volume." That is a Lee–Yang question.

  • They already prove a zero-free statement in the source variables: the per-ball generating function has no zeros on a polydisc whose radius does not depend on the volume, obtained from reflection positivity through the chessboard estimate, with no decoupling.
  • On our side, a convergent polymer expansion gives log Z / |Λ| analytic in a complex neighbourhood of the coupling, uniformly in the volume. In our strong-coupling window, that answers their question there.
  • Our v0.44 work certified zero-freeness of a 2D SU(2) strip on |Im z| < 0.5 by interval arithmetic with winding number zero. The same certificate style could map a zero-free region for small 4D boxes as x increases into the middle.

Use: a zero-free region that persists as x grows is a concrete, computable proxy for "no phase transition in the middle," but it is not equivalent to a mass gap or to entry into this particular polymer basin. Small-box computations also do not establish volume-uniform zero-freeness.

Machinery both already use

Shared tools you can borrow today

One positivity engine

Their Lemma 3.7 proves link reflection positivity by expanding ex Re tr W in characters with non-negative coefficients. Their Lemma 6.4 shows the source-free transfer operator factors as K₀ = CP = PCP ≥ 0 because the plaquette weight is a positive-definite class function. That is the same argument as our ACTION-RP-HEAT-KERNEL-1 and the v0.97 gauge-projected character block. Their argument uses only that the weight is a positive-definite class function, so it carries over to the heat kernel unchanged.

Trace inequality

Lemma 6.4: for mirror-periodic sources, 0 ≤ Zkn(W) ≤ Zn(W)k⁴. Remark 6.6 adds convexity: a bound on the pressure drop between two adjacent tori controls the drop to every larger torus. It needs only reflection positivity and a positive-definite plaquette weight, so it carries over to our heat-kernel lattice and gives an independent check on our volume-uniform endpoint constants.

Chessboard estimate

Lemma 3.8 builds the group generated by face reflections of a cell grid; it acts simply transitively on cells. The chessboard estimate then gives |⟨F⟩| ≤ ⟨∏c′ F[c′]⟩1/|C| for a cell observable F, with complex F allowed. That is a volume-free way to bound one local observable by a fully periodic copy of itself, and a second route to our endpoint constants.

A 4D benchmark for Track B

Clause (0)(d) gives the slope any weak-coupling coarse-graining must reproduce: xi − xj = (j − i)c₀ + o(j − i). That is the 4D counterpart of our exact 2D benchmark. A tensor-RG activity flow that misses this slope at weak coupling should not be trusted inside the middle.

A non-triviality observable

Clause (iv) gives a two-sided law for the action-density two-point function at separation d, proportional to g(d)⁴. Tracking the same correlator through a Track B flow is a second benchmark: it should fall like (log 1/d)⁻² at short distance and, if MIDDLE-1 holds, cross over to exponential decay at long distance.

Matching status ledgers

Their book marks every statement as proved, cited, "proof incomplete at (∗)", "proof outstanding" with a route, or proved only in a model. That lines up with our locked-obligations ledger almost one to one. A shared vocabulary would let a reader trace a joint chain without guessing which side carries which risk.

Structure of their hypotheses

How Theorem 0 chooses its constants, and why it stops where it does

Theorem 0 runs under hypotheses (H1)–(H7) with a strict order of quantifiers:

∃ exponents (d, N) ∃ L₀(N) ∀ L ≥ L₀ odd ∃ Bałaban's parameters p (fixed order) ∃ γ ∃ γ_H ∀ g ∈ (0, γ_H] ∀ m ≥ m_T(g₋(g)) ∀ g₀ ∈ (0, g₋(g)] : K = K(g₀, g)
  • Every smallness is chosen last. Their text says no condition is a lower bound on g, g₀ or any running coupling. Every coupling condition is "small enough". That is the shape of a weak-coupling theorem, and it is the structural reason the method cannot enter the middle: crossing it needs statements that hold at couplings bounded below.
  • Nothing is evaluated. L₀(N) is "one existential constant, never evaluated", and the thresholds γJ, γW, γvi, γU, γΛ are existential. A joint theorem eventually needs at least an explicit upper end for the middle. Asking which thresholds can be made explicit is a natural first request.
  • The windows are non-empty by construction. Finitely many parameters sit in windows such as M₁ < M₂ = L²M₁ < M, made non-empty by earlier lower bounds; the companion account proves the order has no cycles.
  • One exponent they had to fix. Bałaban leaves an exponent q free ("integers greater than 1"); they impose q ≥ 21p₀ + 1 for the second-order extraction under clause (iv).
  • Our side is the opposite. τ is fixed, every constant is a computed decimal, and the open problem is the reach in coupling. The two constant styles are compatible: an explicit theorem at strong coupling can be the last step after an existential one at weak coupling.
Precedent

The QED3 precedent and the chamber trick

The repo's QED3 manuscripts (130 and 263 pages) construct volume-first continuum laws of the field strength for noncompact lattice QED in three dimensions, with the Osterwalder–Schrader axioms, and clustering in almost every translation-ergodic component. The longer version claims non-Gaussian laws and an explicit growth bound of factorial type in n.

  • How they get rotation invariance. The fermions sit on the chambers of the cubic reflection group, one site per chamber. That choice makes the lattice measure reflection positive across diagonal planes as well as coordinate planes. Two reflection directions give the spectral bound H ≥ |P|. With Gaussian domination and dF = 0, that bound forces every moment to be invariant under continuous rotations (the 18-page note, Theorem A).
  • Why it is not yet in YM4. Theorem A is written for laws of closed two-forms, an abelian field strength. Theorem 0 instead gets O(4) only under the flux hypothesis of clause (vi). Their README names "winning O(4)-invariance via an RP trick" as one of the arguments they want to show in simpler settings first.
  • Where our machinery fits. Whether a chamber-type regularization of a non-abelian gauge field can be reflection positive across the diagonal plane t + x = 0 is a finite, checkable question on small lattices. Our character-block code computes exactly this kind of reflected Gram matrix and its smallest eigenvalue.

Smaller items

Side ideas and housekeeping

  • Ground-state simplicity on a small torus. Their Outlook describes a gap and finite-multiplicity ground level on R × T³ but no simplicity. At any fixed cutoff the gauge-projected transfer kernel is a Haar integral of strictly positive plaquette weights, so it is strictly positive and continuous, and Jentzsch's theorem gives a simple top eigenvalue. The open part is keeping that uniform as the cutoff is removed. Our character-block code computes this gap at fixed cutoff.
  • Three routes to their factorial growth bound. They prove volume-free constants of type (n!)cCn with a grid independent of n, and say the Wightman growth condition then reduces to bookkeeping. A polymer expansion of our kind can give n-point bounds with explicit n dependence in the strong-coupling window (our paper currently states two-point bounds), which would cross-check that bookkeeping where both apply.
  • Observable bridge. Their observable is O(f) = Σp f(x(p))(1 − Re tr U(∂p)), a smeared single-plaquette Wilson loop. It is already in our observable class, since a plaquette is a bounded local Wilson loop. This identifies the bare bounded observable at fixed lattice spacing. The continuum renormalized action density needs cutoff-dependent normalization and possibly contact subtractions; estimates for its insertions must remain uniform after those operations.
  • Closing R06 in our paper. At finite spatial volume, spin networks span a dense gauge-invariant subspace by Peter–Weyl and gauge averaging. They are orthonormal for the Haar kinematic inner product, not generally for the interacting measure. The attached note extends density to local cylinder observables at infinite spatial volume and proves the abstract gap implication. Identifying this time-zero space with the physical transfer Hilbert space remains an explicit obligation before closing OS-TRANSFER-GAP-BRIDGE-1. Their §4.4 reconstruction from smeared curvature observables is the continuum analogue.
  • One constant to reconcile. The paper prints mblock ≥ 0.039582864090969, while −log(0.961190174350780) = 0.0395829974455, the value in the OS-bridge bundle. Both are valid lower bounds; the paper should use one.
  • Stated scope for SU(N). Theorem 0 is SU(2); clauses (0)(a)–(c) and (i) are written for every N ≥ 2 in the companion account, and they expect compact semisimple groups to work. Our fragment is SU(2) through its admissibility rules. Both programs would meet the same group-specific steps at SU(3).

Limits of this map

How much weight these links can bear

The repo's own README calls its contents "slop in progress" and raw LLM output, and says the authors cannot tell how reliable the reported progress is. Theorem 0 is conditional on inputs declared clause by clause in their §9; proofs attributed to the companion account were not all supplied; U1, U3 and U5 are withheld and U4 and U6 are not written; and fifteen of Bałaban's printed but unproved statements are used as given.

The original map reported reading the 153-page YM4 draft, rotation note, openings of both QED3 manuscripts and targeted companion sections. This update inspected the public YM4 draft’s normalization and Outlook, searched all three companion TeX files for the finite-size criterion, and checked the part III outstanding-proof list. It did not re-audit the full manuscripts or the unavailable strong-coupling bundles. Every bridge above inherits the conditional status of the clauses it touches. B3 depends on a statement found described in the Outlook; its precise formulation and threshold were not located in this targeted search. The Wilson frontier is now certified for the specified coefficient sums. Its use as a clustering theorem and physical gap bound remains conditional on the original expansion and transfer hypotheses.
